# HONORING THE 50TH ANNIVERSARY OF UCSF FRESNO EMERGENCY MEDICINE

**Speaking:** Costa, Jim (C001059) [D-CA]

```
    HONORING THE 50TH ANNIVERSARY OF UCSF FRESNO EMERGENCY MEDICINE

                                 ______
                                 

                             HON. JIM COSTA

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                        Friday, November 1, 2024

  Mr. COSTA. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor an extraordinary 
milestone: the 50th anniversary of the UCSF Fresno Emergency Medicine 
Residency Program.
  Fifty years ago, visionary physicians recognized the urgent need for 
dedicated emergency care. They established one of the first Emergency 
Medicine residency programs in the United States, transforming the 
landscape of medical training and emergency response. As Dr. James 
Comes aptly noted, ``They had the vision to start an Emergency Medicine 
program here, and it has made all the difference in the world for the 
community.
  The UCSF Fresno Emergency Medicine residency program is now regarded 
as one of the most sought-after programs in the country, attracting top 
medical school graduates. Residents train at Community Regional Medical 
Center, one of California's busiest emergency departments, handling 
upwards of 350 patients a day. This high-volume environment equips 
residents with the skills necessary to excel in their future careers.
  Over the past five decades, the program has pioneered numerous 
innovations, including the Parkmedic program, which allows residents to 
serve as EMS directors in national parks, and the establishment of the 
Wilderness Medicine Fellowship--only the fourth of its kind in the 
Nation. These initiatives exemplify the program's commitment to 
advancing emergency medicine not just locally, but nationally.
  In addition to its educational achievements, the program has made 
significant contributions to emergency medical services in Fresno 
County. Dr. Gene Kallsen, a former chief of the Department of Emergency 
Medicine, was instrumental in reforming ambulance services, ensuring 
faster response times and better patient care. His legacy, and that of 
many others, continues to shape the future of emergency care in our 
region.
  Today, the UCSF Fresno Emergency Medicine residency program stands as 
a model of excellence in medical education and emergency response. It 
has evolved into a fully staffed program, ensuring that residents 
receive comprehensive training in various subspecialties while 
providing critical care to our community.
  As we celebrate this milestone, let us recognize the dedicated 
faculty, residents, and leaders who have contributed to the program's 
success. Their commitment to training skilled clinicians who serve 
diverse and vulnerable populations is a testament to the program's 
enduring impact.
  Mr. Speaker, I invite my esteemed colleagues to join me in 
celebrating the UCSF Fresno Emergency Medicine residency program and 
its invaluable contributions to our community and to the field of 
emergency medicine.
```
