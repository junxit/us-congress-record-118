# RECOGNIZING THE COURAGEOUS SERVICE OF DEPUTY GREGORY MAY

**Speaking:** Griffith, H. Morgan (G000568) [R-VA]

```
        RECOGNIZING THE COURAGEOUS SERVICE OF DEPUTY GREGORY MAY

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. H. MORGAN GRIFFITH

                              of virginia

                    in the house of representatives

                        Friday, November 1, 2024

  Mr. GRIFFITH. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the courageous 
service of Craig County Sheriff's Deputy Gregory May.
  In September of 2024, Hurricane Helene ravaged much of Virginia's 
Ninth Congressional District. Craig County was one of many counties 
under severe distress from conditions created by Helene.
  On September 27, Deputy May was called into action to respond to an 
emergency situation involving an injured person in the Sinking Creek 
area of Craig County. Despite facing heavy rains and high winds, Deputy 
May, along with Craig County Emergency Services and Fire Department 
personnel, attempted to execute a daring rescue mission.
  While assisting in the rescue operation, Deputy May was struck by 
flying debris. This incident inflicted serious bodily injury, requiring 
him to receive immediate medical attention and care. He sustained a 
concussion, a broken jaw, and a severe laceration under his neck.

[[Page E1102]]

  This emergency situation called for bravery and sacrifice. Deputy May 
answered that call in the face of this unprecedented hurricane, knowing 
full well that his life would be endangered.
  Deputy May's outstanding service to the Craig County Sheriff's Office 
and the people of Craig County is commended by the Craig County Board 
of Supervisors, who value the ``indispensable role of all law 
enforcement officers, firefighters, rescue squads, and medical 
responders'' to protect their community.
  Craig County first responders have a dedicated serviceman among their 
ranks. Deputy May's story is a model of sacrifice and service that 
inspires his colleagues and community.
  As a locality of roughly 5,000 people in a mountainous area of 
Virginia, Craig County residents can be assured that their security and 
well-being are considered essential, thanks to first responders like 
Deputy May.
```
