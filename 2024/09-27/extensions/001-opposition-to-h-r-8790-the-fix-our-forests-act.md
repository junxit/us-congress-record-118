# OPPOSITION TO H.R. 8790, THE FIX OUR FORESTS ACT

**Speaking:** Hoyle, Val T. (H001094) [D-OR]

**Measures:** H.R. 5169, H.R. 8790

```
            OPPOSITION TO H.R. 8790, THE FIX OUR FORESTS ACT

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. VAL T. HOYLE

                               of oregon

                    in the house of representatives

                       Friday, September 27, 2024

  Ms. HOYLE of Oregon. Mr. Speaker, this week I voted against H.R. 
8790. I support parts of this bill. For example, it would create a 
Community Wildfire Risk Reduction Program, in line with a 
recommendation from the non-partisan Wildland Fire Mitigation and 
Management Commission. It would also increase tribal inclusion in 
forest management.
  But I cannot overlook the unfunded mandates in H.R. 8790. For 
instance, I strongly support creating a Community Wildfire Defense 
Research Program within the Joint Fire Science Program (JFSP) to 
provide research that would improve the wildfire-resistance of 
structures and communities. However, JFSP is already oversubscribed and 
the bill ignores the Wildland Fire Mitigation and Management 
Commission's recommendation to significantly increase the program's 
funding. H.R. 8790 would greatly expand JFSP's responsibilities without 
a corresponding increase in funding, resulting in less applied research 
being provided to land managers. This is not the way to address the 
wildfire crisis.
  I am also concerned about what is not in H.R. 8790. Over 100 
recommendations from the Wildland Fire Mitigation and Management 
Commission were not addressed, including many recommendations to 
support and expand the workforce. You cannot talk about the wildfire 
crisis without talking about the firefighters who do body-breaking work 
to protect our forests and communities. It is unacceptable that 
Congress has not passed a permanent solution to the firefighter pay 
cliff. There should be no question that federal wildland firefighters 
deserve at least $15 an hour and a daily pay supplemental when they are 
working on long-duration wildfires, prescribed fires, or deployed away 
from their duty station to a high-risk fire area. I am a cosponsor of 
bipartisan legislation, H.R. 5169, the Wildland Firefighter Paycheck 
Protection Act, which would finally ensure that these heroes are paid 
well for their work. This bill should have been included within H.R. 
8790.
  For these reasons, I do not believe H.R. 8790 is sufficient for the 
communities I represent that have been experiencing record-setting 
wildfire seasons and expect Congress to approach the issue with the 
seriousness it deserves.
  I will be sending a letter urging Senate leadership to improve the 
bill and send it back for reconsideration in the House. I want to 
achieve a better outcome that can solve problems and make a difference 
in our forests. I stand ready to work with my colleagues on both sides 
of the aisle to achieve that goal.

                          ____________________
```
