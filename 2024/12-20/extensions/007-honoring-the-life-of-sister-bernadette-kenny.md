# HONORING THE LIFE OF SISTER BERNADETTE KENNY

**Speaking:** Griffith, H. Morgan (G000568) [R-VA]

```
              HONORING THE LIFE OF SISTER BERNADETTE KENNY

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. H. MORGAN GRIFFITH

                              of virginia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Friday, December 20, 2024

  Mr. GRIFFITH. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor the life of Sister 
Bernadette Kenny, who passed away on December 7, 2024.
  ``Sister Bernie'' came into the world on December 8, 1938. She was 
born in Boston, Massachusetts, and raised in Massachusetts. Her 
Catholic faith and interest in caring for others resulted in her 
joining the Medical Missionaries of Mary at age 19. She went to study 
nursing at Ireland's Lady of Lourdes Hospital.
  After completing her training as a nurse and midwife, she served 
several missions in Africa, which included stints in Tanzania, Ethiopia 
and the Central African Republic.
  In 1978, Sister Kenny moved to Southwest Virginia. Soon thereafter, 
she founded the Health Wagon in Norton, Virginia.
  Southwest Virginia is a sprawling rural area, and access to health 
care services has always been a challenge for the local communities. 
For many people, it takes hours of driving to simply reach a hospital.
  Sister Kenny founded the Health Wagon to address this challenge head 
on. The Health Wagon consisted of a Volkswagen Beetle filled with 
medical tools and supplies. Sister Kenny traveled in her Beetle 
throughout the coalfields region to meet the health care needs of the 
local communities.
  She administered services to struggling residents free of charge in 
her quest to heal their bodies as well as heal their souls. She 
underscored the emotional aspects to health care, saying ``in most 
health care the emotional part is ignored.'' She added: ``health care 
is social; it's a way of identifying what's inside a person.''
  Thanks to Sister Kenny's support, the Remote Area Medical (RAM) 
project, a popular and overwhelmingly successful venture that delivers 
free health care to various underserved communities in America, has 
hosted clinics in Southwest Virginia.
  Because of Sister Kenny's strong leadership and fierce advocacy, the 
Health Wagon has sustained operations to this very day. The group takes 
pride in providing compassionate, quality health care to the medically 
underserved people in the mountains of Appalachia.
  The indispensable endeavor to deliver vital health care to 
Appalachian communities, including the region's coal miners, would not 
have been so emphasized today if it were not for Sister Kenny. I join 
the people of Southwest Virginia and all of Appalachia in mourning her 
loss, but the lasting impact she had on the region will forever be 
cherished.
```
