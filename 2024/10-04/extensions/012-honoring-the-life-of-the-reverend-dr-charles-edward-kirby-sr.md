# HONORING THE LIFE OF THE REVEREND DR. CHARLES EDWARD KIRBY, SR.

**Speaking:** McGarvey, Morgan (M001220) [D-KY]

```
    HONORING THE LIFE OF THE REVEREND DR. CHARLES EDWARD KIRBY, SR.

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. MORGAN McGARVEY

                              of kentucky

                    in the house of representatives

                        Friday, October 4, 2024

  Mr. McGARVEY. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor and recognize the 
remarkable life of Reverend Dr. Charles Edward Kirby, Sr., a true 
servant of our community and an extraordinary and iconic Louisvillian.
  Reverend Kirby was born in 1928 in Trigg County, Kentucky, ``a real 
country place,'' and for nearly a century, he lived a life dedicated to 
serving his Lord and the people of Kentucky. After coming to Louisville 
and with the support of just a handful of dedicated followers, Reverend 
Kirby answered a divine call and established the work of his life, the 
Southern Star Missionary Baptist Church, in the 1950s. Under his 
leadership, the church quickly grew to 34 members and secured its first 
permanent building, but Reverend Kirby knew he wanted to expand his 
reach, and he made Southern Star the first church to broadcast live 
services on WLOU Radio.
  Reverend Kirby was called to a still higher mission, and he became 
one of Louisville's great civil rights leaders. He joined 10,000 
Kentuckians, alongside Martin Luther King Jr., in a 1964 demonstration 
in Frankfort advocating for equal public accommodations for Black 
Americans, and Southern Star Missionary Baptist Church served as a 
vital meeting place for civil rights groups in Louisville throughout 
the civil rights movement. Reverend Kirby spoke out against injustice 
wherever he saw it, and he held a number of demonstrations against 
investments in Apartheid South Africa and used his voice and his 
influence to attend open housing marches in Louisville where he, side 
by side with other community leaders, marched into white neighborhoods 
for multiple nights in 1967. Eventually, the Kentucky Fair Housing Act 
was passed.
  In 1972, his deep commitment to service led him to establish the 
Martin Luther King Free Food Store, a pioneering food distribution 
program run from his church. He was recognized by Louisville and 
Jefferson County for feeding thousands of people citywide, and he even 
received an Outstanding Community Service Award. Years later, Reverend 
Kirby became president of the Kentucky Southern Christian Leadership 
Conference, from which he received the Chapter of the Year Award and a 
Leadership Award.
  Reverend Kirby's influence extended beyond his church and civil 
rights work. He began recording gospel music in 1961 and produced 
records well into the 1980s. His gospel classic ``Lord Come On,'' was 
recorded

[[Page E1012]]

live right at Southern Star, and his album ``God is Real'' made its way 
onto digital streaming platforms in the 2000s.
  Reverend Kirby was born to sharecroppers who worked for just a dollar 
a day. He was born in a state and country that did not consider him 
fully, equally human. He did not let that stop him from changing 
Louisville and the Commonwealth of Kentucky. A believer in the promise 
of a life in paradise after this imperfect life on Earth, Reverend 
Kirby sang from the pulpit, ``come on, and let's go to that land where 
I am bound.'' Go on, Reverend Kirby, to your eternal rest.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask the House of Representatives to join me in 
honoring the life and legacy of Reverend Dr. Charles Edward Kirby Sr. 
His leadership, compassion, and service will continue to inspire, and 
his memory will live on in the hearts of those who were touched by his 
work, his voice, and his unwavering commitment to justice.
```
