# HONORING THE 50TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE CENTRAL VALLEY BLACK NURSES

**Speaking:** Costa, Jim (C001059) [D-CA]

```
    HONORING THE 50TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE CENTRAL VALLEY BLACK NURSES

                                 ______
                                 

                             HON. JIM COSTA

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, October 22, 2024

  Mr. COSTA. Mr. Speaker, today, we gather to commemorate a remarkable 
milestone in the field of healthcare and community service--the 50th 
Anniversary of the Central Valley Black Nurses Association (CVBNA). 
This historic occasion is a testament to the enduring legacy and 
unwavering commitment of the CVBNA to improving healthcare access and 
equity.
  Founded in 1973 under the visionary leadership of Dr. Carol Drake, 
former Dean and Professor of Nursing at Fresno City College. along with 
other trailblazing founders, Brenda Starks, Odester Tate, Gladys 
Hollie, Ruth Ann Terry, and Loretta Leach-Scruggs, the CVBNA has 
consistently exemplified excellence in nursing and community advocacy. 
In 2016, the CVBNA achieved a significant milestone by becoming an 
official chapter of the National Black Nursing Association, Inc. 
(NBNA). Subsequently, on March 24, 2017, the CVBNA was incorporated in 
the state of California as a non-profit organization, solidifying its 
commitment to the advancement of healthcare and service to the 
community.
  The CVBNA's mission is both clear and commendable: to investigate, 
define, and address the healthcare needs of Black Americans and other 
minorities, striving to ensure healthcare commensurate with that of the 
larger society. With their profound understanding, knowledge, interest, 
concern, and experience, Black nurses in the CVBNA have consistently 
made a significant impact on the healthcare status of the Black 
community. One of the hallmarks of the CVBNA is its unwavering 
commitment to community service. Through voluntary hours, members 
provide an array of preventative health screenings and health 
education, encompassing many critical areas. These services are 
delivered in collaboration with a multitude of community-based 
partners, including faith-based organizations, civic groups, fraternal 
organizations, hospitals, and schools of nursing.
  The CVBNA's community outreach efforts have left an indelible mark on 
the Central Valley. During the pandemic, they were on the front lines, 
providing COVID-19 vaccinations and contributing countless hours to 
vaccinate thousands of individuals on the westside of Fresno. Their 
commitment to health education is unwavering, with numerous pop-up 
events for high blood pressure screening and comprehensive health 
education in underserved communities. They've also extended their care 
to homeless students, providing hygiene kits, and expanded their 
presence to underserved schools. Moreover, they've been mentors and 
educators, supporting student nurses and tutoring those facing academic 
challenges.
  The vision of the CVBNA extends beyond the present day. Their 
advocacy efforts are rooted in the promotion of culturally competent, 
accessible, and affordable healthcare. They ardently champion the 
professional and educational advancement of ethnic nurses and remain 
steadfast in educating consumers, healthcare professionals, and 
policymakers about the unique health challenges faced by ethnic 
minority populations. Their dedication to developing ethnic minority 
nurse leaders in areas of health policy, practice, education, and 
research is unwavering. As we stand on the cusp of the CVBNA's 50th 
Anniversary, we must recognize and celebrate this momentous occasion. 
It represents five decades of unwavering dedication, advocacy, and 
service to the community.
  Mr. Speaker, I invite my esteemed colleagues to join me in honoring 
the Central Valley Black Nurses Association. Let us celebrate their 
legacy and their ongoing commitment to advancing healthcare access and 
equity for all.
```
