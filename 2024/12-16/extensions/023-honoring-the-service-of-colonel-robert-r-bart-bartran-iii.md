# HONORING THE SERVICE OF COLONEL ROBERT R. "BART" BARTRAN, III

**Speaking:** Guest, Michael (G000591) [R-MS]

```
    HONORING THE SERVICE OF COLONEL ROBERT R. ``BART'' BARTRAN, III

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. MICHAEL GUEST

                             of mississippi

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, December 16, 2024

  Mr. GUEST. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the service of 
Colonel Robert R. ``Bart'' Bartran, III. COL Bartran is a fellow 
Mississippian who has dedicated his life to serving our country and the 
great state of Mississippi.
  COL Bartran was appointed as the Director of the Counterdrug Program 
for the Mississippi National Guard (MNG) in August of 2021, where he 
also served as the Commandant of the Regional Counterdrug Training 
Academy (RCTA) on Naval Air Station Meridian. He relinquished the 
responsibilities as Commandant of the RCTA in December of 2023 but 
continues to serve as the Counterdrug Coordinator (CDC) for the MNG to 
this date. In these roles, COL Bartran has led with distinction the 
critical counterdrug training program for the National Guard and 
Federal, state, and local law enforcement across Mississippi, Alabama, 
Georgia, Louisiana, and Tennessee.
  COL Bartran graduated from West Jones High School in 1988 and 
attended Jones County Junior College, graduating with a Bachelor of 
Science Degree in Criminal Justice from the University of Southern 
Mississippi. He then completed his Master's Degree at Liberty 
University in Lynchburg, Virginia. COL Bartran valued continuing 
education and graduated from the Mississippi Law Enforcement Officer 
Training Academy in Jackson, Mississippi.
  Prior to being selected for the Active Guard and Reserve Program, COL 
Bartran was a police officer with the City of Ridgeland Police 
Department and the City of Laurel Police Department. His civilian law 
enforcement career

[[Page E1275]]

that spanned nearly a decade culminated with service as the Senior 
Narcotics Agent for the Jones County Sheriff's Department in Laurel, 
Mississippi.
  COL Bartran entered the service on September 17, 1988, as a Private 
assigned to Service Battery, 3rd/83rd Field Artillery in the United 
States Army Reserves in Laurel, Mississippi. In August of 1991 he 
transferred to the 2/112th Armored Brigade in the Texas National Guard 
with duty in Fort Worth, Texas. In 1993, COL Bartran transferred back 
to the Great State of Mississippi and became a member of the National 
Guard. Assigned to the 184th Transportation Brigade, shortly followed 
by movement into the 112 Military Police Battalion, COL Bartran pursued 
his dream of becoming a Military Police Officer. On January 26, 1995, 
he graduated from Federal Officer Candidate School at Fort Benning, 
Georgia, and was commissioned into the Mississippi Army National Guard 
as a Second Lieutenant in the Military Police Corps.
  In February of 1995, COL Bartran attended Military Police Officer 
Basic Course and was assigned to the 113th Military Police Company. For 
the next 8 years, COL Bartran would serve in multiple Military Police 
Officer positions to include Platoon Leader, Detachment Commander, and 
Company Commander of the 114th Military Police Company where he would 
deploy twice back-to-back in that capacity. The culminating event as a 
Military Police Officer for COL Bartran was in August of 2018 when he 
was selected as the Battalion Commander for the 112th Military Police 
Battalion in Canton, Mississippi, and served honorably in that position 
until February of 2021.
  Upon his return from the second deployment, COL Bartran would take on 
a series of Staff Positions serving as a Maintenance Officer, Personnel 
Officer, Training Officer, Operations Officer, Plans Officer, Executive 
Officer, and then a Deputy Commander before being appointed as the 
Deputy Inspector General for the Mississippi National Guard. The 
majority of the aforementioned positions were performed in a full-time 
status after being hired into the Active Guard and Reserve System in 
April of 2004. COL Bartran has served at every echelon in the state 
from the platoon level all the way up to the State Headquarters 
Primary/Special Staff and a Brigade Command Equivalent.
  COL Bartran's Awards include the Bronze Star Medal, Meritorious 
Service Medal with 1 Silver Oak Leaf Cluster and one Bronze Oak Leaf 
Cluster, Army Commendation Medal with 1 Bronze Oak Leaf Cluster, Army 
Achievement Medal with 2 Bronze Oak Leaf Cluster, Army Reserve 
Component Achievement Medal with 1 Silver Oak Leaf Cluster and 1 Bronze 
Oak Leaf Cluster, National Defense Service Medal with 1 Bronze Star, 
Afghanistan Campaign Medal with 2 Bronze Stars, Global War on Terrorism 
Expeditionary Medal, Global War on Terrorism Service Medal, Armed 
Forces Service Medal, Humanitarian Service Medal with Numeral 2, Armed 
Forces Reserve Medal with Silver Hourglass Device and M Device and 
Numeral 5, Non Commissioned Officer Professional Development Ribbon, 
Army Service Ribbon, Overseas Service Ribbon with Numeral 4, Army 
Reserve Component Overseas Training Ribbon with Numeral 2, North 
Atlantic Treaty Organization Medal, North Atlantic Treaty Organization 
Medal (International Security and Assistance Forces), Joint Meritorious 
Unit Award with 1 Bronze Oak Leaf Cluster, Army Superior Unit Award, 
Mississippi Magnolia Cross, Mississippi Magnolia Medal with 1 Bronze 
Oak Leaf Cluster, Mississippi Commendation Medal, Mississippi War Medal 
with 1 Bronze Star, Mississippi Emergency Service Medal with 3 Bronze 
Oak Leaf Clusters, Mississippi Counterdrug Service Medal, Mississippi 
Service School Medal, Mississippi Longevity Medal with 1 Silver Oak 
Leaf Cluster, German Armed Forces Proficiency Badge, Inspector 
General's Badge, and the coveted Air Assault Badge. Additionally, to 
COL Bartran's credit are five deployments over the course of his 35-
year career, taking him overseas to Germany, Cuba, and multiple tours 
in Afghanistan.
  I am thankful for Colonel Bartran's exceptional service to our 
country and the state of Mississippi, and I wish him all the best in 
his retirement.
```
