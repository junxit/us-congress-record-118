# IN REMEMBRANCE OF SERGEANT ARTHUR O. McNITZKY

**Speaking:** Burgess, Michael C. (B001248) [R-TX]

```
             IN REMEMBRANCE OF SERGEANT ARTHUR O. McNITZKY

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. MICHAEL C. BURGESS

                                of texas

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 19, 2024

  Mr. BURGESS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor U.S. Army Sergeant 
Arthur Othello McNitzky. Arthur was the first Denton County soldier to 
die in combat in World War I. His

[[Page E1168]]

call to service and sacrifice made a lasting impact on multiple 
generations.
  McNitzky was raised in Denton, Texas and answered the Nation's call 
to service, enlisting in the U.S. Army on June 30, 1917. He was listed 
as a Private in Company M, 142nd Infantry Regiment, 36th Division, U.S. 
Army. After enlisting, McNitzky was sent for training to Camp Bowie in 
Fort Worth.
  McNitzky was killed in action during the Battle of Argonne Forest in 
St. Etienne, France. The Battle of the Argonne Forest, also known as 
the Meuse-Argonne Offensive, is recognized as a major part of the final 
Allied offensive of World War I. The Battle of Argonne Forest was hard 
fought over an agonizing 47 days, from September 26, 1918 until 
November 11, 1918.
  For his valiant service in action, Sergeant McNitzky was awarded the 
Croix de Guerre by the French government. His mother, Emma McNitzky, 
and her daughter, Rosa, traveled to France to claim Arthur's body and 
bring his remains home. He was buried beside his father, August 
McNitzky, and shares a headstone with him in the McNitzky family plot 
in the I.O.O.F. Cemetery in Denton, Texas.
  Sergeant McNitzky's selfless sacrifice is to be respectfully 
reflected upon and remembered more than one hundred years later. We are 
forever grateful to the many men and women who have served and continue 
to serve in our nation's armed forces.
  On Veterans Day 2024, Judge Forrest C. Beadle, Denton County Criminal 
Court No. 3, and Commander of American Legion Post 71, presented me 
with a United States flag that had been planted at Sergeant McNitzky's 
gravesite. The label reads ``SGT Arthur O. McNitzky The American 
Legion, Post 71 Denton, Texas Remembrance Day October 8, 1918-October 
8, 2018''. STILL SERVING. It is my honor to recognize Sergeant 
McNitzky's sacrifice and the continuing service of Denton's Post 71, in 
honor of Sergeant McNitzky, with donation of this special American flag 
to the Denton County Office of History and Culture.
```
