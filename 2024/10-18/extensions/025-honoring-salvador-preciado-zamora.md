# HONORING SALVADOR PRECIADO ZAMORA

**Speaking:** Thompson, Mike (T000460) [D-CA]

```
                   HONORING SALVADOR PRECIADO ZAMORA

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. MIKE THOMPSON

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                        Friday, October 18, 2024

  Mr. THOMPSON of California. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor 
Salvador Preciado on his 85th birthday. Mr. Preciado is a pillar of our 
community, having revolutionized grape growing through his techniques 
of grafting vines.
  Mr. Preciado grew up in El Grullo, Mexico. In 1963, Mr. Preciado 
immigrated to the United States from Mexico. He began working with 
Annie and Frank Emmolo at their plant nursery, where he was first 
introduced to grafting. Mr. Preciado then fell in love with grafting, 
and this led him to start his own company. Simultaneously, Mr. Preciado 
attended language classes to help strengthen his English. Today, Mr. 
Preciado resides in St. Helena and is regarded as one of the most 
successful grafters in the wine community.
  Mr. Preciado has had a thriving career in the wine community. He has 
taught grafting around the world, including South America, Europe, and 
throughout the United States. Mr. Preciado's hard work created many job 
opportunities within the wine community. The lessons he shared with 
fellow grafters have been spread throughout the word's wine community. 
Mr. Preciado has helped shape many vineyards in Napa, Sonoma, and in 
other countries.
  Mr. Preciado is a strong community servant, guided by the desire to 
help others. In the 1980s he was given the title, ``The Vine 
Whisperer'' as Mr. Preciado proved essential when he used his grafting 
skills to save vineyards during the phylloxera outbreak in Sonoma and 
Napa Counties. Mr. Preciado and his crew have worked tirelessly to 
graft infected vines and preserve local vineyards. In response, the 
University of California, Davis invited Mr. Preciado to help identify 
vines in their vineyards because of his impressive abilities. Mr. 
Preciado was also recognized for his work in the ``Latino Winemakers 
Oral History Project Collection'' of the Smithsonian National Museum of 
American History Kenneth E. Behring Center.
  Mr. Preciado's wife, Gloria, also gives back to the community. Mrs. 
Preciado was recognized in 2001 by the Dalai Lama for the work she did 
in the St. Helena community as well as in El Grullo. In Mexico, she 
worked to help build a local drug and alcohol rehabilitation center and 
a soup kitchen for underserved children. Mr. and Mrs. Preciado also 
helped start a local soccer team in St. Helena so people from El Grullo 
could feel connected to their hometown and to each other. They maintain 
an open door, often hosting community dinners and inviting community 
members and friends to stay in their home.
  Mr. Preciado has positively impacted our community. Therefore, it is 
fitting and proper that we honor Mr. Preciado here today.
```
