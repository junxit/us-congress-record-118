# HONORING BRANDON STAGLIN, GAREN STAGLIN, SHANNON STAGLIN, AND SHARI STAGLIN AND ONE MIND

**Speaking:** Thompson, Mike (T000460) [D-CA]

```
  HONORING BRANDON STAGLIN, GAREN STAGLIN, SHANNON STAGLIN, AND SHARI 
                          STAGLIN AND ONE MIND

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. MIKE THOMPSON

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                        Friday, October 18, 2024

  Mr. THOMPSON of California. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor 
Brandon Staglin, Garen Staglin, Shannon Staglin, and Shari Staglin, the 
founders of One Mind, in recognition of the 30th One Mind Music 
Festival for Brain Health, the organization's annual premier brain 
health charity event.
  One Mind was founded by the Staglins in 1995 to address the gaps they 
identified in brain health research and patient support in the wake of 
Garen and Shannon's son, Brandon's, diagnosis of schizophrenia. That 
same year, the Staglins hosted a small concert fundraiser at their Napa 
vineyard, to support the burgeoning organization and its ambitious 
goals. Since this first iteration, the One Mind Music Festival for 
Brain Health has evolved to become one of the most important 
fundraising events for brain health in the country, raising over $600 
million over the years to support invaluable research and initiatives 
revolutionizing the mental health field.
  With the money it fundraises through its music festival, One Mind has 
been able to play an integral role in pushing the brain health field 
forward. In 2003, One Mind partnered with the National Institute of 
Mental Health (NIMH) to launch the North American Prodrome Longitudinal 
Study, leading to the discovery of biomarkers enabling the prediction 
of psychosis onset in clinical high-risk youth with 70 percent 
accuracy. One Mind was also the principal fundraiser for the 2021 Ken 
Burns documentary ``Hiding in Plain Sight: Youth Mental Illness'', 
which was screened at the White House and the U.S. Capitol and has 
aired on 350 PBS stations. Furthermore, One Mind has been instrumental 
in launching the careers of boundary-pushing scientists with their 
Rising Star Awards, recognizing the likes of Dr. Kafui Dzirasa, a 
pioneer of the brain pacemaker, and Dr. Joshua Gordon, the director of 
NIMH from 2017 to 2024.
  Outside of One Mind, the Staglins have continued to commit themselves 
to lives of service. In 2008, Shari and Garen advocated alongside U.S. 
Representative Patrick Kennedy to pass the Mental Health Parity and 
Addiction Equity Act of 2008, a bill mandating fair and equal insurance 
coverage for physical and mental health. In 2009, Shari, Garen, and 
Brandon collaborated with actor Glenn Close to launch the nonprofit 
Bring Change to Mind, an organization dedicated to ending the stigma 
surrounding mental health. Additionally, Shari and Garen chaired UCLA's 
Centennial Fundraising Campaign, leading the campaign to raise over 
$5.49 billion to support the school and its programs.
  Mr. Speaker, the Staglins have left an indelible mark on mental 
health in this country. Their philanthropic efforts and continued 
support of scientific innovation in the brain health field through 
their One Mind organization deserves recognition. Therefore, it is 
fitting and proper that we honor them here today.
```
